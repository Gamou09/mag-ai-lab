# Meeting Notes

Turn raw meeting material (transcripts, rough notes, chat logs) into a clear summary that someone who missed the meeting can read quickly and act on.

## Output format

Use this structure. If a section has no content, write "None" rather than removing it or inventing content.


# Meeting Notes — [Meeting title]
**Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD] · **Time:** [Start–End, timezone]
**Attendees:** [Name (role)], [Name (role)]
**Absent:** [Name]

## Purpose
[One or two sentences on why the meeting took place.]

## Key discussion points
- **[Topic]:** [Main points raised, arguments, figures mentioned.]
- **[Topic]:** [...]

## Decisions
- [What was agreed, stated as a fact.]

## Action items
| # | Action | Owner | Due |
|---|--------|-------|-----|
| 1 | [Verb-first task] | [Name] | [Date] |

## Open questions
- [Issues raised but not resolved.]

## Next steps
1. [What happens next, in order.]
2. [Next meeting: date, time, purpose, if one was set.]


## Guidelines

**Stay faithful to the source.** The notes are a record, so include only what was actually said. If an owner, date or detail is missing, write `TBC` instead of guessing, because people act on these notes and a wrong owner or date causes more harm than a blank one.

**Separate decisions from discussion.** A decision is something the group agreed to do or adopt. Ideas that were suggested but not agreed belong under discussion points or open questions. Mixing the two misleads readers about what was settled.

**Make action items actionable.** Start each one with a verb, give it a single owner and a due date. If a task has several owners, split it or name the lead, so responsibility is clear.

**Distinguish action items from next steps.** Action items are individual tasks with an owner. Next steps give the bigger picture: the sequence of what happens after the meeting and where the work is heading. Keep next steps short, usually 2–5 points.

**Be concise and scannable.** Remove filler, small talk and repetition. Use short bullets. Keep names, numbers, dates and technical terms exactly as stated.

**Match the meeting's language.** Write the notes, headings included, in the language the meeting was held in, unless the user asks for another language.

**Flag uncertainty.** If part of a transcript is unclear or a speaker can't be identified, mark it `[unclear]` rather than filling the gap.

**Keep the order logical.** Group discussion points by topic, not by the order people spoke, so the reader sees each subject in one place.
